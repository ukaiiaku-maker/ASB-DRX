## 2026-10-13T23:46:00Z — Arrhenius Hall–Petch source distinction

- Goals: G01, G04, G05.
- Prediction: boundary/TJ-only sources and bulk-random sources will generate
  different organization because their site measures, stress amplification,
  and event strain differ even under the same Arrhenius barrier family.
- Experiment: preserve the common hazard form while comparing (i) pile-up
  transmission/absorption, with stress concentration scaling as
  `sqrt(d/l_dd)` and site multiplicity scaling as `rho/d`, (ii) GB/TJ
  nucleation, with stress concentration scaling as `sqrt(d/b)` and boundary
  site measure scaling as `1/d^2`, and (iii) a declared spatially random bulk
  source control with the same barrier parameters.
- Result: the supplied Azizi–Dillon paper supports the first two closures as
  distinct fixed-state Arrhenius mechanisms; it does not prescribe their
  post-event structural evolution. That transition kernel remains a V66 model
  requirement.
- Limitation: the paper's example maps are structural demonstrations rather
  than a calibration for the present generic BCC fixture.
- Next branch: trace the production source/sink and boundary-transfer paths,
  then implement one shared event-rate interface with separate site fields and
  content ledgers.

- Goals: G01, G02, G05, G06.

  reaction capable of absorption--rotation or network maturation.

  been exercised under a controlled unload/hold protocol in V66.
- Next branch: energy-complete reduced H1 absorption and H2 conservative
  structural-network redistribution, with sign-balanced and incompatible
  controls.

## 2026-10-13T23:43:00Z — reduced H1/H2 and source-location decisions

- Goals: G01, G04, G05, G06.
- Prediction: only crystallographically compatible net signed absorption can
  change a simple tilt-boundary misorientation; Read--Shockley concavity can
  drive conservative redistribution between neighboring boundaries; GB/TJ
  source localization should differ from a matched random-source control.
- Experiment: execute an exact straight-boundary content/energy transaction,
  a three-region common-axis redistribution, and two 48 x 48 common-transport
  calculations whose site-weight histograms and integrated Arrhenius source
  are identical but spatial locations differ.
- Result: compatible positive absorption increased misorientation by 0.1 deg
  and lowered the reduced Helmholtz energy by 7.85 mJ/m2. Sign-balanced and
  incompatible controls changed no structural content. A +/-0.1 deg network
  perturbation lowered boundary energy by 0.133 mJ/m2 in either direction,
  with exact signed-content closure; an accommodation stiffness above 87.54
  J/m2/rad2 stabilized the 2.5 deg symmetric state. GB/TJ sources produced a
  long-wave source-aligned band (structure-factor index 2 and boundary
  correlation 0.9999); randomly relocated identical site weights produced a
  short-wave field (index 11.18 and boundary correlation 0.0148).
- Limitation: the structural law is a straight low-angle scalar reduction.
  The pair-source transition kernel has not yet passed a complete external
  work/heat transaction. The selected H1 rate is often EXP-floor saturated,
  so its assumed activation segment does not yet provide an independently
  qualified temperature sensitivity.
- Next branch: production interface-state coupling followed by a retained-state
  deformation/fixed-strain/unloaded-anneal discriminator.

## 2026-10-14T00:05:00Z — production common-axis dynamic/static reaction

- Goals: G01, G04, G05, G06.
- Prediction: an inherited compatible pile-up can mature a planar LAGB under
  the same stationary reaction during loading and after removal of the applied
  stress; continued source activity can either replenish or frustrate net
  structural content depending on sign balance.
- Experiment: compose the Arrhenius boundary source, authoritative common-wall
  mechanics/heat residual, and energy-qualified absorption--rotation operator
  into one production interval. Prepare one 2 deg inherited boundary and fork
  its exact retained state into continued-deformation and zero-stress branches.
- Result: the retained 2.25670 deg boundary matured to 2.56046 deg during
  continued deformation and 2.56202 deg during the zero-stress hold. Both
  branches closed line balance and used the same structural law. Continued
  sign-balanced production raised final line density and slightly suppressed
  net maturation relative to static recovery. No orientation was assigned:
  every structural increment followed `delta theta=b delta Lambda`.
- Limitation: this is a planar common-axis inherited-subgrain test. It does not
  create a new internal boundary or include arbitrary 3-D Frank--Bilby
  compatibility. The applied-stress removal is a zero-stress reduced fork, not
  yet the full multigrain work-ledgered unload protocol.
- Next branch: determine whether selective growth through an orientation
  gradient can concentrate multiple LAGBs into a viable HAGB.

## 2026-10-14T00:12:00Z — orientation-gradient maturation and SIBM handoff

- Goals: G01, G02, G04, G05, G06.
- Prediction: a lower-stored-energy inherited subgrain in a cumulative
  orientation gradient can consume neighbors, eliminate LAGBs, and sum their
  compatible misorientations without creating or imposing an orientation.
  Recovery should arrest this route when it removes contrast faster than the
  front migrates; continued stored work can maintain it.
- Experiment: evolve a 13-cell 1-D network with 2.5 deg orientation steps under
  the production EXP-floor front law, exact bulk plus Read--Shockley energy,
  exact cell-disappearance events, a fixed Arrhenius recovery law, and fixed
  stored-work law over 650--1000 K and 0, 0.1, and 1/s. Handoff a mature case
  to the existing complete-energy SIBM graph operator.
- Result: the target's pre-existing 15 deg orientation survived while ten
  neighboring cells disappeared, producing two 15 deg interfaces and a 6.5
  micrometre target. Static 650 K matured and was front-viable over 50 s;
  static 900/1000 K recovered and arrested; deformation-maintained cases
  matured across most conditions, while 1000 K at 0.1/s remained below HAGB.
  The 1000 K, 1/s handoff advanced the existing front 0.647 micrometres in 1 s
  with nonincreasing energy. No new orientation or grain label was created,
  and the subgrain count decreased. Time refinement from 0.1 to 0.025 s passed
  the provisional 5% target-width criterion.
- Limitation: parameters are documented generic assumptions, not a material
  calibration; geometry is a 1-D cellular reduction; spatial-grid convergence
  and tensorial production-owner integration remain open.
- Next branch: embed this maturation/handoff state transition in the multigrain
  owner/front path, then exercise a nontrivial coupled DRX/ASB case.
