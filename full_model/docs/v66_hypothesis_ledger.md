## 2026-10-13T23:46:00Z — Arrhenius Hall–Petch source distinction

- Goals: G01, G04, G05.
- Prediction: boundary/TJ-only sources and bulk-random sources will generate
  different organization because their site measures, stress amplification,
  and event strain differ even under the same Arrhenius barrier family.
- Experiment: preserve the common hazard form while comparing (i) pile-up
  transmission/absorption, with stress concentration scaling as
  `sqrt(d/l_dd)` and site multiplicity scaling as `rho/d`, (ii) GB/TJ
  nucleation, with stress concentration scaling as `sqrt(d/b)` and boundary
  site measure scaling as `1/d^2`, and (iii) a declared spatially random bulk
  source control with the same barrier parameters.
- Result: the supplied Azizi–Dillon paper supports the first two closures as
  distinct fixed-state Arrhenius mechanisms; it does not prescribe their
  post-event structural evolution. That transition kernel remains a V66 model
  requirement.
- Limitation: the paper's example maps are structural demonstrations rather
  than a calibration for the present generic BCC fixture.
- Next branch: trace the production source/sink and boundary-transfer paths,
  then implement one shared event-rate interface with separate site fields and
  content ledgers.

## 2026-10-13T23:50:00Z — active-operator audit

- Goals: G01, G02, G05, G06.
- Prediction: V65 stores interfacial content but lacks a structural-content
  reaction capable of absorption--rotation or network maturation.
- Experiment: trace the actual V65 production path and classify each candidate
  mechanism as represented, enabled, and exercised.
- Result: EXP-floor glide, Taylor resistance, signed bulk transport, heat, and
  moving-front transfer are all active. Interface line and residual Burgers

- Limitation: the static zero-rate recovery path is represented but has not yet
  been exercised under a controlled unload/hold protocol in V66.
- Next branch: energy-complete reduced H1 absorption and H2 conservative
  structural-network redistribution, with sign-balanced and incompatible
  controls.

## 2026-10-13T23:43:00Z — reduced H1/H2 and source-location decisions

- Goals: G01, G04, G05, G06.
- Prediction: only crystallographically compatible net signed absorption can
  change a simple tilt-boundary misorientation; Read--Shockley concavity can
  drive conservative redistribution between neighboring boundaries; GB/TJ
  source localization should differ from a matched random-source control.
- Experiment: execute an exact straight-boundary content/energy transaction,
  a three-region common-axis redistribution, and two 48 x 48 common-transport
  calculations whose site-weight histograms and integrated Arrhenius source
  are identical but spatial locations differ.
- Result: compatible positive absorption increased misorientation by 0.1 deg
  and lowered the reduced Helmholtz energy by 7.85 mJ/m2. Sign-balanced and
  incompatible controls changed no structural content. A +/-0.1 deg network
  perturbation lowered boundary energy by 0.133 mJ/m2 in either direction,
  with exact signed-content closure; an accommodation stiffness above 87.54
  J/m2/rad2 stabilized the 2.5 deg symmetric state. GB/TJ sources produced a
  long-wave source-aligned band (structure-factor index 2 and boundary
  correlation 0.9999); randomly relocated identical site weights produced a
  short-wave field (index 11.18 and boundary correlation 0.0148).
- Limitation: the structural law is a straight low-angle scalar reduction.
  The pair-source transition kernel has not yet passed a complete external
  work/heat transaction. The selected H1 rate is often EXP-floor saturated,
  so its assumed activation segment does not yet provide an independently
  qualified temperature sensitivity.
- Next branch: production interface-state coupling followed by a retained-state
  deformation/fixed-strain/unloaded-anneal discriminator.

## 2026-10-14T00:05:00Z — production common-axis dynamic/static reaction

- Goals: G01, G04, G05, G06.
- Prediction: an inherited compatible pile-up can mature a planar LAGB under
  the same stationary reaction during loading and after removal of the applied
  stress; continued source activity can either replenish or frustrate net
  structural content depending on sign balance.
- Experiment: compose the Arrhenius boundary source, authoritative common-wall
  mechanics/heat residual, and energy-qualified absorption--rotation operator
  into one production interval. Prepare one 2 deg inherited boundary and fork
  its exact retained state into continued-deformation and zero-stress branches.
- Result: the retained 2.25670 deg boundary matured to 2.56046 deg during
  continued deformation and 2.56202 deg during the zero-stress hold. Both
  branches closed line balance and used the same structural law. Continued
  sign-balanced production raised final line density and slightly suppressed
  net maturation relative to static recovery. No orientation was assigned:
  every structural increment followed `delta theta=b delta Lambda`.
- Limitation: this is a planar common-axis inherited-subgrain test. It does not
  create a new internal boundary or include arbitrary 3-D Frank--Bilby
  compatibility. The applied-stress removal is a zero-stress reduced fork, not
  yet the full multigrain work-ledgered unload protocol.
- Next branch: determine whether selective growth through an orientation
  gradient can concentrate multiple LAGBs into a viable HAGB.

## 2026-10-14T00:12:00Z — orientation-gradient maturation and SIBM handoff

- Goals: G01, G02, G04, G05, G06.
- Prediction: a lower-stored-energy inherited subgrain in a cumulative
  orientation gradient can consume neighbors, eliminate LAGBs, and sum their
  compatible misorientations without creating or imposing an orientation.
  Recovery should arrest this route when it removes contrast faster than the
  front migrates; continued stored work can maintain it.
- Experiment: evolve a 13-cell 1-D network with 2.5 deg orientation steps under
  the production EXP-floor front law, exact bulk plus Read--Shockley energy,
  exact cell-disappearance events, a fixed Arrhenius recovery law, and fixed
  stored-work law over 650--1000 K and 0, 0.1, and 1/s. Handoff a mature case
  to the existing complete-energy SIBM graph operator.
- Result: the target's pre-existing 15 deg orientation survived while ten
  neighboring cells disappeared, producing two 15 deg interfaces and a 6.5
  micrometre target. Static 650 K matured and was front-viable over 50 s;
  static 900/1000 K recovered and arrested; deformation-maintained cases
  matured across most conditions, while 1000 K at 0.1/s remained below HAGB.
  The 1000 K, 1/s handoff advanced the existing front 0.647 micrometres in 1 s
  with nonincreasing energy. No new orientation or grain label was created,
  and the subgrain count decreased. Time refinement from 0.1 to 0.025 s passed
  the provisional 5% target-width criterion.
- Limitation: parameters are documented generic assumptions, not a material
  calibration; geometry is a 1-D cellular reduction; spatial-grid convergence
  and tensorial production-owner integration remain open.
- Next branch: embed this maturation/handoff state transition in the multigrain
  owner/front path, then exercise a nontrivial coupled DRX/ASB case.

## 2026-10-14T00:45:00Z — common-state coupled DRX/ASB exposure

## 2026-10-14T01:20:00Z — retained-state unload and static reactivation

- Goals: G01, G02, G04, G06.
- Prediction: if the target's dynamic recession is caused by the instantaneous
  common mechanical affinity rather than irreversible loss of the RX pathway,
  unloading should reverse the front and a zero-rate hold should sustain it.
- Experiment: continue the exact 7.2% loading checkpoint through 900 complete
  reverse-rate intervals to zero imposed shear strain, then hold that strain
  for 6 microseconds with zero imposed rate. Preserve residual stress, heat,
  signed defects, owners, and all front history.
- Result: the target first receded to -0.807% relative to its loaded size while
  positive stress relaxed, then reversed as residual stress changed sign and
  reached +0.596% at zero imposed strain. During the subsequent zero-work hold,
  edge 10--20 selected target growth in all 300 intervals and the target grew
  monotonically to +3.279%, a further +2.683% after unloading. All 2120 total
  front events were accepted; maximum front closure was 5.72e-7 and maximum
  mechanical first-law residual was 0.331%.
- Numerical repair: the first attempted zero-work hold exposed an invalid
  relative normalization: finite defect/elastic-to-heat turnover has nearly
  zero net internal change and exactly zero work, so dividing its truncation
  residual by the net change reports 100% at every timestep. The signed
  residual was not changed. Its relative audit now uses the independently
  evaluated gross component turnover, and a dissipative zero-work regression
  test verifies the same 5% criterion.
- Limitation: zero imposed strain retains -1.679 GPa residual shear stress and
  is therefore not called stress-free annealing. The horizon is 6 microseconds
  on a 16 x 16 mechanism grid; long static completion and spatial refinement
  remain open.
- Next branch: discriminate residual-stress-assisted static growth from a true
  zero-mean-stress anneal, then integrate or reject a source-location closure
  using a complete work/heat transaction.

- Result: mean stress first fell below 10 MPa at interval 38 and ended at

  remained 0.331% and 5.72e-7. The controller extracted 1.81e-13 J net work.
- Limitation: zero *mean* stress is not zero local stress, the target increment

  It does, however, reject the hypothesis that the observed static direction
  requires a nonzero mean residual shear stress.
- Next branch: make the Hall--Petch source hypothesis energy-complete by tying
  line creation to its conjugate slip and stored-energy/heat transaction.

## 2026-10-14T02:15:00Z — conjugate Hall--Petch source implementation

- Goals: G01, G04, G05.
- Prediction: a boundary or random source can be admitted without invented
  energy if each idealized grain-spanning pair event creates one line of each
  sign per `d^2` and simultaneously sweeps `b/d` plastic shear. Opposite line
  signs then preserve net Burgers content while their conjugate shear adds.
- Implementation: use the published Hall--Petch Arrhenius hazard for event
  frequency, the geometric `1/d^2` line increment (not an arbitrary density
  increment), and the immutable EXP-floor barrier. Compute `tau delta gamma`
  independently, subtract exact local defect-storage increase, deposit only
  the nonnegative remainder as heat, and qualify the full elastic/defect/
  thermal common energy at fixed total strain.
- Atomic result: signed line, total line, plastic-distortion, and Mura--Nye
  ledgers close. A stressed event is downhill and closes complete energy;
  an unstressed thermal attempt is rejected because it cannot pay line storage.
  The operator is integrated as an optional common-interval channel for both
  GB/TJ-only and exactly matched random-site modes; the baseline remains off.
- Limitation: the grain-spanning pair geometry is a declared reduced source
  closure, not identified by the fixed-state Hall--Petch rate paper or DDD.
  It must be treated as an ablation until its coupled spatial consequences are
  discriminated without retuning.
- Next branch: run matched common-state GB/TJ and random source exposures from
  the same matured handoff and compare organization, RX front, and heat.

## 2026-10-14T03:10:00Z — production source-location discriminator

- Goals: G01, G03, G04, G05.
- Prediction: exact source-location matching should preserve integrated source
  opportunity while producing source-aligned density increments; if location
  is a leading RX/ASB mechanism, the GB/TJ and random cases should also diverge
  macroscopically over the same physical horizon.
- Experiment: run source-disabled, GB/TJ-only, and exact-permutation random
  cases from the identical matured common state through 4% shear at 1000 K and
  4e4/s. No amplitude or constitutive parameter was retuned. Twenty early
  intervals remained inactive because their event energy was below the
  independently computed complete-energy resolution; the following 480
  source intervals were energy qualified.
- Result: GB/TJ and random cases accumulated 13.3787 and 13.3782 expected
  events, only 3.59e-5 relative difference after nonlinear feedback. Source
  transactions created 4.645e12 line-cell content and generated about
  1.5e-17 J heat, with maximum first-law residuals 1.79% and 2.60%. The
  source-induced density increment correlated 0.938 with GB/TJ sites in the
  boundary case and 0.891 with its permuted sites in the random case; the
  random increment correlated only 0.079 with boundaries. Nye perturbations
  also differed (0.652 versus 1.450 1/m RMS).
- Macroscopic decision: location changed the spatial perturbation but not the
  response family over this horizon. GB-minus-random stress differed by only
  535 Pa, target-growth effects differed by 4.94e-10, and their density-field
  difference was 2.82e-6 of baseline RMS. The physically derived source is
  therefore retained as an optional ablation, not promoted as a wall or RX
  nucleation forcing mechanism.
- Numerical note: attempts whose complete transaction energy is below the
  independently calculated floating-point energy resolution are now marked
  inactive rather than divided by an unresolved near-zero scale. Resolved
  events retain the unchanged 5% hard closure requirement.
- Next branch: improve the primary H1/H2 route by carrying explicit structural
  boundary content and Frank compatibility with the migrating owner geometry,
  then test whether growth changes misorientation by conservation rather than
  by a prescribed owner angle.
